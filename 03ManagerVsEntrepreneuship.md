# Difference between Entrepreneur and Manager

> Exam-oriented notes | Subject: Entrepreneurship / Principles of Management

---

## 1. Introduction

**Entrepreneur:** A person who identifies a business opportunity, organises resources (men, money, materials, machinery), and sets up a new enterprise, while bearing the risk and enjoying the profit or loss.

**Manager:** A person appointed by the owners of an enterprise to plan, organise, direct, and control the work of others in order to achieve organisational goals. A manager works within an already established enterprise.

**Key idea:** The entrepreneur *creates* the business; the manager *runs* it.

## 3. Points Elaborated (For Long-Answer Questions)

### 3.1 Sets up a new enterprise vs. runs an existing one
The entrepreneur starts from nothing: he finds an idea, arranges finance, hires people, and launches the business. The manager joins after the business exists and is responsible for keeping it running smoothly and profitably.

### 3.2 Innovator vs. Administrator
- **Innovator (Entrepreneur):** introduces new products, new methods of production, new markets, new sources of raw material, or new organisational forms (as explained by **Joseph Schumpeter**).
- **Administrator (Manager):** implements policies, supervises staff, ensures rules are followed, and maintains order and efficiency.

### 3.3 Accountability
- The entrepreneur is his own boss, so he is answerable only to himself (and to the market, law, and society).
- The manager reports to senior managers, the board of directors, and ultimately the shareholders or owners.

### 3.4 Continuous vs. occasional functions
- Entrepreneurial functions such as idea generation, project formulation, launching, and restructuring are performed **only when required**, for example when starting a new venture, diversifying, or revitalising a declining business.
- Managerial functions such as planning, organising, staffing, directing, and controlling are performed **every day**, without break.

### 3.5 Risk bearing
- The entrepreneur invests money and effort, and the outcome may be profit or total loss. Risk-bearing is his defining characteristic.
- The manager gets his salary whether the business earns profit or loss, so he is not exposed to financial risk (though he may face job-related consequences for poor performance).

## 4. Additional Points of Distinction

- **Creation vs. maintenance:** Entrepreneurs create value; managers maintain and optimise value.
- **Change vs. stability:** Entrepreneurs bring change and disrupt; managers bring stability and control.
- **Vision vs. execution:** Entrepreneurs provide vision and direction; managers convert the vision into results.
- **Role in economy:** Entrepreneurs create jobs, wealth, and economic development; managers help organisations use resources efficiently.
- **Failure impact:** An entrepreneur may lose his entire investment; a manager may lose his job but not his capital.

---

## 5. Similarities Between Entrepreneur and Manager

1. Both work towards the success of the enterprise.
2. Both need leadership and decision-making skills.
3. Both coordinate human and material resources.
4. Both need communication and interpersonal skills.
5. In small businesses, the same person often plays both roles (the **owner-manager**).

## 7. Quick Memory Tricks (Mnemonics)

- **Entrepreneur = "Creator, Risk-taker, Owner, Innovator"** (**CROI**)
- **Manager = "Employee, Salary, Administrator, Continuous"** (**ESAC**)
- One-line rule: *"The entrepreneur builds the ship; the manager sails it."*

## 10. Conclusion (Use to Finish an Answer)

Though the two roles overlap, especially in small businesses, they are fundamentally different. The **entrepreneur** is the founder, innovator, and risk-taker who creates the enterprise, while the **manager** is a salaried professional who administers and runs it efficiently. A successful organisation needs **both**: entrepreneurial vision to start and grow, and managerial discipline to sustain and control.
