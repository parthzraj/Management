
# Management Notes

Exam-oriented notes for studying the principles, theories, and practice of
management. The notes combine lecture coverage, textbook clarifications,
comparisons, model answers, and quick revision material.

## Contents

### Foundations of management

1. [Introduction to Management and Organizations](./01Introduction_to_Management_Exam_Notes.md)
   Meaning, characteristics, dimensions, objectives, effectiveness, and
   efficiency.
2. [Importance, Nature, and Levels of Management](./02Principles_of_Management_Lecture_2_Exam_Notes.md)
   The importance of management, management as a science and an art, and the
   top, middle, and lower levels of management.
3. [Manager vs. Entrepreneur](./03ManagerVsEntrepreneuship.md)
   A detailed comparison of roles, responsibilities, risk, rewards, authority,
   and decision-making.
4. [Managerial Roles and Skills](./04Principles_of_Management_Managerial_Roles_and_Skills_Notes.md)
   Mintzberg's ten managerial roles and Katz's technical, human, and
   conceptual skills.
5. [Evolution of Management](./05EvolutionOfManagement.md)
   The development of management thought from scientific management through
   modern approaches.

### Organizations, planning, and decision-making

## Suggested study order

1. Start with the [introduction notes](./01Introduction_to_Management_Exam_Notes.md).
2. Study the [importance, nature, and levels of management](./02Principles_of_Management_Lecture_2_Exam_Notes.md).
3. Review [managerial roles and skills](./04Principles_of_Management_Managerial_Roles_and_Skills_Notes.md).
4. Read the [evolution of management](./05EvolutionOfManagement.md).
5. Use the [manager vs. entrepreneur comparison](./03ManagerVsEntrepreneuship.md) for distinction-based questions.
6. Finish with [business organization, planning, and decision-making](./06rest.md).

## How to use these notes

- Read the topic overview and learning objectives before each chapter.
- Use the comparison tables and quick revision sheets for last-minute review.
- Practise the model questions in each chapter as written-answer preparation.
- Check the coverage or transcription notes where the source lecture was
  incomplete or unclear.

## Scope and source notes

These are study notes, not an official textbook or syllabus. Several chapters
are based on auto-transcribed lecture subtitles; unclear passages are marked
or clarified where appropriate. Always compare the material with your
instructor's syllabus and recommended references.
